Summary

Metallobiochemist working at the experiment–theory–simulation interface, with research spanning protein design, enzyme mechanisms, and single-atom–resolution spectroscopy. Experienced in integrating spectroscopy, computation, and molecular biology to resolve metal-centered reactivity in biological systems and to design functional metalloproteins.

Experience

  • Postdoctoral Research Fellow, University of Texas at Austin — Austin, TX, USA (2021–present)
    Laboratory of Dr. Yi Lu – green biochemistry and enzyme design

    • Simulated O₂ binding across several hundred heme protein homologues; applied machine-learning voxel representations to predict enzyme reactivity and metal binding in designed structures. Presented at the Texas Advanced Computing Center User Meeting (2023).
    • Designed novel metallo-DNAzymes that mimic heme peroxidase enzymes.
    • Developed and characterized next-generation model systems for heme–copper oxidase, sulfite reductase (types A and D), and nitrogenase.
    • Engineered DNA-aptamer–based delivery systems for fluorescent cargo across blood–brain barrier model systems.
    • Designed kinetic experiments to resolve competing pathways and extract rate behavior.
    • Built Python-based workflows for processing, modeling, and comparing experimental datasets.
    • Refined reaction conditions and assay parameters to improve robustness and reproducibility.

  • Graduate Research Assistant, University of California, Irvine — Irvine, CA, USA (2015–2021)
    Laboratory of Dr. Michael T. Green – heme enzyme reactivity

    • Established that peroxidase Compound II is an unprotonated Fe(IV)=O species using a multi-spectroscopic approach, resolving a long-standing mechanistic debate.
    • Simulated NRVS spectra of heme enzyme intermediates to validate experimental assignments.
    • Reprocessed neutron crystallography datasets using electron diffraction maps, identifying sample-damage artifacts responsible for conflicting literature interpretations.

  • Undergraduate Research Assistant, University of Central Florida — Orlando, FL, USA (2013–2015)
    Laboratory of Dr. Stephen J. King – neurodegenerative disease models

    • Behavioral studies in a novel mouse model of Charcot–Marie–Tooth neuropathy, including statistical analysis and scripting.
    • 3D confocal microscopy and quantitative morphology analysis of murine neuromuscular junctions.
    • Developed fluorescence-based assays to quantify intracellular transport and trafficking in neuronal systems.
    • Performed quantitative imaging and MATLAB-based analysis of cellular distribution and dynamics.
    • Conducted dose-response experiments to evaluate toxicity and transport behavior of small molecules.
